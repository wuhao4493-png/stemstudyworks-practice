How to use this algebra check

Allow about 10–15 minutes. There are two questions each on linear equations, expanding and factorising, quadratics, simultaneous equations and inequalities. Try each without looking at a worked solution, then select Mark my answers to see your score and useful next steps. Your selections stay in this browser; nothing is sent to a server.

Algebra questions

Linear equations · 1 of 10
Solve 4x − 7 = 21.

Add 7 to both sides: 4x = 28. Divide by 4 to get x = 7.

Linear equations · 2 of 10
Solve 3(x − 2) = 2x + 5.

Expand: 3x − 6 = 2x + 5. Subtract 2x and add 6: x = 11.

Expanding & factorising · 3 of 10
Expand −2(3x − 4).

Multiply −2 by both terms: (−2)(3x) = −6x and (−2)(−4) = +8.

Expanding & factorising · 4 of 10
Factorise x² + 5x + 6.

Find two numbers with product 6 and sum 5: 2 and 3. The result is (x + 2)(x + 3).

Quadratics · 5 of 10
Solve x² − 9x + 20 = 0.

Factorise: (x − 4)(x − 5) = 0, so x = 4 or x = 5.

Quadratics · 6 of 10
Solve x² + 6x + 9 = 0.

Factorise: (x + 3)² = 0. Therefore x = −3 (a repeated root).

Simultaneous equations · 7 of 10
Solve x + y = 9 and x − y = 3.

Add the two equations: 2x = 12, so x = 6. Then y = 9 − 6 = 3.

Simultaneous equations · 8 of 10
Solve 2x + y = 11 and x − y = 1.

Add the equations: 3x = 12, giving x = 4; substitute into x − y = 1 to find y = 3.

Inequalities · 9 of 10
Solve −3x < 12.

Divide by −3 and REVERSE the inequality sign: x > −4.

Inequalities · 10 of 10
Solve 2x + 5 ≤ 17.

Subtract 5: 2x ≤ 12. Divide by 2: x ≤ 6.

Algebra diagnostic: frequently asked questions

Which GCSE Maths topics does this check cover?

Ten selected practice questions across five algebra topics: linear equations, manipulating and factorising expressions, quadratics, simultaneous linear equations, and solving inequalities. It does not cover every GCSE algebra skill.

Can a teacher use this as a starter or revision checkpoint?

Yes. It is a short informal check for independent work or classroom revision. Use each missed question to decide which skill to revisit and supplement it with a broader assessment before making grading decisions.

Why does the inequality sign sometimes reverse?

When dividing or multiplying both sides of an inequality by a negative number, reverse the comparison sign. For example, −3x < 12 becomes x > −4.

Can I print the questions without answers?

Yes. Use your browser's print function. The print layout hides worked explanations, buttons and the score report.
