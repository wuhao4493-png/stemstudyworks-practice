How to expand and factorise expressions

Expanding brackets means replacing a product with a sum of terms. For example, −3(2x − 5) = −6x + 15, because the negative coefficient multiplies both the 2x and the −5. Double brackets follow the same principle: multiply each term in the first bracket by each term in the second, then collect like terms.

Factorising is the reverse operation

For common factors, 6x + 12 = 6(x + 2). For monic quadratics, look for two numbers whose sum is the coefficient of x and whose product is the constant. For example, x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3), since 2 + 3 = 5 and 2 × 3 = 6.

Checking your factorisation
